Interpreting the findings

The correlation of +0.77 indicates a strong positive relationship: ZIP codes with higher smoking percentages also tend to have higher percentages of residents reporting frequent mental distress.

For every one-percentage-point increase in smoking prevalence, the model predicts approximately a 0.4912-percentage-point increase in frequent mental distress. Two neighborhoods differing by ten percentage points in smoking prevalence would therefore differ by approximately 4.9 percentage points in predicted distress prevalence.

The p-value is below 0.001, indicating that the association is statistically significant under the model.

Important limitation: This is an area-level association, not proof that smoking causes mental distress. It cannot establish an individual-level relationship, and other neighborhood characteristics may help explain the observed pattern.
