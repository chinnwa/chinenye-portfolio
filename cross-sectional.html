What cross-sectional data can and cannot tell you?

“As young adults have moved through their twenties over the past decade, their anxiety has steadily worsened. A generation is becoming more anxious as it ages.”

- From the National Health Statistics Center.

They drew a random sample of about 27,000 adults yearly, each one independent from year to year (no one is surveyed twice). They completed the interview once, asking questions regarding symptoms and health status.

This claim assumed that as young adults moved through their 20s, they are getting more anxious, but the design never established whether the passage of time caused anxiety to increase or if differences between age groups explain the pattern.

This is a cross-sectional study, which means it captures a snapshot of different people at one point in time. It can show that anxiety is associated with age or year, but it cannot tell us whether getting older caused anxiety to rise. The data do not follow the same people over time.
