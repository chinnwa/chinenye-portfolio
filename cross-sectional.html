Week 04 / Study design

What cross-sectional data can and cannot tell us

PH 320 · Applied Biostatistics in Public Health · CUNY York College

“As young adults have moved through their twenties over the past decade, their anxiety has steadily worsened. A generation is becoming more anxious as it ages.”

Claim attributed in the original assignment to the National Health Statistics Center.

Understanding the study

Researchers drew a random sample of about 27,000 adults each year. Each annual sample included different people, and participants completed a single interview about symptoms and health status.

What the data can show

These repeated cross-sectional samples can reveal differences in reported anxiety across age groups and survey years. They can help identify population-level patterns worth investigating.

What the data cannot show

The claim assumes that the same young adults became more anxious as they moved through their twenties. However, the study did not follow the same individuals over time. It therefore cannot establish that aging itself caused anxiety to rise or distinguish that explanation from differences between age groups and survey periods.

Key takeaway: Cross-sectional evidence can describe associations and population trends, but it cannot by itself demonstrate individual change over time or establish causation.
